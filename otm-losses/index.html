Trying not to lose money might be
the reason you're not making any.
Most traders believe the best trade is
the one that never loses,
but there's a difference between avoiding losses
and managing risk.
Imagine a trader who refuses to take
a loss.
They move their stop loss,
cut their winners early,
and skip valid setups because they're scared
of being wrong.
They're protecting the account they have,
but they're also limiting what it could
become.
Because some losses aren't really losses,
they're the cost of doing business.
A trade with no stop loss can
empty your account.
But a planned one percent loss on
a valid setup is just the price
of reaching the next 3R winner.
Of course,
taking losses doesn't automatically make you profitable.
A bad trade is still a bad
trade.
The important part is understanding the difference
between losing money to a gamble and
losing money to a plan.
Because consistent traders aren't the ones who
never lose,
they're the ones who know which losses
to take.
