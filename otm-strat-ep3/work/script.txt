[hook] Last episode, I asked: price wicks below an old low, then closes back above it. Breakdown, or liquidity sweep? [answer] The answer is a liquidity sweep.
[what] Liquidity is where stop losses and pending orders pile up. [bsl] Above old highs sits buy-side liquidity. [ssl] Below old lows sits sell-side liquidity. [eq] Equal highs and equal lows hold the most.
[valid] But not every low is real liquidity. [valid2] In an uptrend, a low only counts if the move from it broke the previous high.
[two] When price reaches liquidity, it does one of two things. [run] A run: it closes through the level and keeps going, usually leaving a fair value gap behind. [sweep] A sweep: it pokes through with a wick, fails to close beyond, and snaps back.
[warn] A sweep is a warning, not an entry. [steps] Wait for the change of character, then a fair value gap or an order block, then the pullback.
[ex] On gold, equal lows sat at thirty-eight ten. Price wicked down to thirty-eight oh-two and closed back above. [ex2] Then it broke the last lower high: change of character. I bought the pullback at thirty-eight eighteen, stop under the sweep at thirty-eight hundred. Target: the buy-side liquidity at thirty-eight seventy. About one to three.
[save] Save this. [s1] Liquidity sits above highs and below lows. [s2] A wick through and a close back inside is a sweep. [s3] Wait for the change of character, then the pullback.
