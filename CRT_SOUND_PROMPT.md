Today I'm going to show you a powerful strategy called CRT. So what is CRT? It stands for Candle Range Theory, and here's how it works.
If price sweeps the high of a candle and then closes back below it, there's a good chance price is heading for the lows.
And if price sweeps the low of a candle and closes back above it, there's a good chance price is going for the highs.
Once we know that, we drop to a lower timeframe and look for an entry that lets us ride that move.

**0:37 (example 1: 4-hour candle, then the 15-minute chart)**
Let's look at an example. Here's a four-hour candle. We wait to see which side price takes first.
Price took the high and closed back below it, so that's our CRT high, and now we expect a move down to the CRT low.
On the fifteen-minute chart we've got a mitigation block and a fair value gap.
The setup is simple: enter at the fair value gap, stop loss just above the swing high, target the CRT low.
And there it is: price went right into the CRT low.

**1:14 (bullish example)**
Now a bullish example. Price swept the previous candle's low, which gives us our CRT low and CRT high.

Price is also sitting inside a fair value gap, and just to the left there's an inverse fair value gap.
Where a fair value gap and an inverse fair value gap overlap, we get a balanced price range.
So we buy from there, stop loss just below the balanced price range, take profit at the CRT high. And take profit was hit.
