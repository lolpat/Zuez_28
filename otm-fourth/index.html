Every trade has two sides,
and the market is always looking for
the side that's being ordered around.
First,
the chaser.
Price runs,
they jump in late,
right at the top,
and become someone else's exit.
Then the hoper.
The trade goes wrong,
so they move the stop and wait
for the market to come back.
It rarely does.
Then the doubter.
A perfect setup arrives at their level.
They hesitate and watch it leave without
them.
Three traders,
three different mistakes,
one reason.
They let the market decide for them.
The fourth trader is different.
They mark the level before price gets
there.
They know where they're wrong before they
enter,
and when price arrives,
they don't react.
They execute.
That's the difference between being ordered by
the market and ordering it.
